## Follow-up — approval-gate audit — 21 Nov 2026

An independent read-only Codex audit of `UseApp`/`CommitGate` (O1), together with a maintainer-side reading of the key dispatch path, found four concrete ways the general hand could cause an effect without a matching approval card. Each was repaired:

| Gap | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| `key enter` was dispatched without any gate, so a form or chat set to send on Enter sent typed words. | Enter with typed words in a field that is not a search box goes through the same gate, card and resume as a Send tap. Search boxes are recognized by whole words in the field's id name, class, hint or label (search/query/suche/url/omnibox); a field showing only its hint counts as empty (`isShowingHintText`), but typing the hint's own word counts as typed. |
| Only a control whose *model-time* words held a commit word was re-read before tapping; a Preview that became Submit during the model call was tapped. | Every tap re-reads only the nodes under the tap point (`ScreenReader.under`, a few IPCs instead of a full tree) and refuses if the element's words or bounds changed. The control that takes the tap (nearest clickable ancestor-or-self) is judged: its own label, short inner words of a small wordless button, and anything drawn on top at that spot. |
| `flatten` cut words at 120 characters before the gate read them, so a long label hid a trailing "Submit". | The gate reads whole words; only the model's line is cut. The card names the commit word ("tap “Submit”?"). |
| A yes matched package, words, session and typed text only, so a Delete approved for one item could delete another item, or carry into a later goal. | A grant is also bound to the normalized goal, the control's bounds and the words on its row. |

Regressions run the real `UseApp.run` route on the isolated Android 17 emulator against new fixture pages (Gate page, Note form, Search form). Eight route regressions for these gaps failed on the prior source and pass after the repair; the search-box case passes on both by design (no new friction). Codex then reviewed the repair diff; its follow-up findings (hint-equal text, substring search matches, the tap-taking parent/wordless button, a row moved under the same button, missing deadline checks after the new reads) were repaired in the same change. Exact suite counts and phone verification are recorded in SYSTEM.md §5.

**Retained limits (not repaired):** a wordless control whose words are long or numerous, or sit off the tap point, is still unlabeled to the gate; a search box that names itself none of the search words asks before Enter (friction, safe), while a non-search field that does name itself so is exempt; an item identified only off its row (for example a detail page under a fixed toolbar Delete) is bound by goal and position, not item identity; a single-field non-message form still needs a top-of-screen name as `to` before a card can be shown; a few milliseconds remain between the fresh read and the gesture; a tap can still hit a node drawn above the element that is not in the accessibility tree. The joined `Agent.choose → RunningFor → run` route is exercised by existing UI/controller tests with a fake hand, not with these fixtures. This narrows O1; it does not make label-based detection complete.

### Browser forms follow-up — 21 Nov 2026

A second read-only Codex audit traced browser use (installed-browser fallback, browser packages and other apps' WebViews all run through the same `UseApp` hand; Misura renders no WebView and Google suggestion HTML is not rendered). It found no approval-forging route. Repaired, with route regressions on the same fixture that failed on the prior source:

- **Enter judged on the whole screen and the focused field.** The Enter check and the gate's fresh read used the model's 150-line screen list, so a filled field further down let Enter through. Both now read the whole screen; Enter is free only when the focused field is a search box, otherwise any typed words on screen ask, bound to the focused field. The final read must still show focus on that field before a yes is spent, so a yes for Enter in form A never presses Enter after focus moves to form B. With typed words on screen, Enter needs focus in a visible field: no focused field, focus in an off-screen field, or focus on a button (where Enter would press it outside the tap checks) presses nothing until the right field is tapped or scrolled into view.
- **Rows keep every word as identity.** The earlier “ticking text” exception was removed: a record named “101”, “08:30” or “10%” stays bound, so another record moved under the same Delete asks again. A row with a live countdown therefore asks again too (friction, safe).
- **Form cards show typed words and take a tap.** With several filled fields, or none named, the card shows the question with the exact typed words (and any verified chat name) in its line, without a label: HomeScreen allows a spoken “go” only for labelled message cards, so pay/delete/form cards stay tap-only.

Codex reviewed this repair and re-checked it; its findings (spoken approval via a new label, unbound or unverified focus including a focused button, time/percent identities, a quadratic regex, a mis-aimed test and stale SYSTEM text) were repaired in the same change.

**Retained (decisions, not repaired):** a web page can label an effectful field “Search” or an effectful button “Continue”, or leave it unlabeled; accessibility metadata cannot prove what a page control does, so the general hand cannot keep ordinary browsing prompt-free and also gate every page action. Web editors that expose no editable flag are invisible to the Enter check. Browser origin and form action are not part of a grant. Treat the general hand on untrusted web pages as model-directed with label-based approval only.

## Storage design and verification update (19 Nov)

- **Field/file encryption chosen over SQLCipher:** small shared helper, no new dependency/native ABI or database-file migration, existing Room query behavior preserved. SQLCipher would cover query metadata and the database file more broadly, but would still need Keystore key management and separate protection for preferences/item files; changing an installed database would add migration risk. It was not added. Query columns (ids/packages/times/states) remain visible. Conversation/seen identifiers use SHA-256 for equality lookups, not encryption; low-entropy guesses remain possible.
- **Availability is intentional:** Keystore/encryption failure permits plaintext writes with a content-free debug warning, rather than disabling capture. Marked ciphertext never falls back to plaintext on a failed tag, unknown version or unavailable key; callers return no value and retain the record. Notes reject writes while unreadable. Existing aliases are never replaced. Lost keys cannot recover older ciphertext. No auth prompt is added to background use.
- **Migration is gradual:** unmarked plaintext JSON/files remain readable and encrypt on the next write; preferences migrate on first read. This is not a secure erase of old database/WAL/free pages, preference files, deleted files or historical backups. Session and controller evidence copied from inbox messages has its own lifetime; inbox retention does not erase those copies.
- **Tests added/updated, not executed:** `StorageCipherTest` (text/binary round trips, random IVs, tag tampering/wrong key/truncation/version rejection, unavailable-key plaintext fallback/warning, retry migration, failed persistence); `RoomStorageTest` (encrypted payloads, legacy rewrite, v2→v3 Room validation and retention backfill, cutoff/late-delivery/insertion pruning, notes/session preservation); `RememberStorageTest` (retains >60 notes, no overwrite while unreadable); `NotificationAccessTest` (default allow, exact block/revoke/unblock, old opt-ins ignored, own-package skip). Existing image fixtures now decrypt before checking pixels; live-eval credential lookup uses protected reads.
- **Checks:** offline `scripts/check-oss.py` passed (224 tracked paths, 218 eligible text files); Four XML files, check-oss/publish-system Python syntax, five shell scripts and `git diff --check` passed. A broader Python 3.9 syntax check hit an existing nested-f-string syntax error in unchanged `build-mockups.py:205`; that design renderer was not changed or run. A standalone JVM smoke check compiled actual `StorageCipher.kt` using the cached Kotlin compiler (no Gradle) and passed text/binary round trips, fresh IVs, tamper/version rejection, unavailable-key plaintext fallback and preference migration/failed-commit preservation. Android framework interfaces used test proxies; the real Keystore provider was not exercised. No Gradle, device, real Keystore, release build or merged-manifest validation. ## Result and needs-you notifications — 22 Nov 2026 (launch sprint M3)

- Source: `notify/SessionAlerts.kt`, hooks `Agent.onSettled` (after the settled session is saved) and `Agent.onAllSent` (every message on a finished card confirmed), wired in `MisuraGraph`; "in front" from `HomeActivity` resume + window focus.
- Navigation only: no actions, no RemoteInput; the tap is an immutable explicit `HomeActivity` PendingIntent carrying the session ID with a per-session data URI. Approve-from-notification is not built.
- Privacy: `VISIBILITY_PRIVATE` with an explicit generic `publicVersion` ("Result ready" / "Misura needs you", "Open Misura to view"). Waiting drafts show only the recipient ("Message to …"), a tool's stopped tap only its label, never the words it would send. The unlocked shade shows the request and first answer line.
- Silent while Home is resumed, focused, the screen interactive and unlocked; coming into Misura clears the shade copies. Accepted edge: Home showing another conversation still counts as in front (the result is on Home/Earlier).
- Each hook event is re-checked against the stored session before posting (same revision and status, not dismissed, not deleted); events are handled one at a time and posting shares a lock with Home's "came in front" clear. Dismiss, delete and failure also report, and a failed send clears its session's alert, so a stale "needs you" or result goes away. "Needs your OK" posts only while every task on the card still waits for approval. Failures keep the existing Problems alert; the "sources but no named result" line posts nothing; an out-of-steps answer that explains unfinished work still posts as a result. A finished card announces "Sent." only while it is still current, idle and not dismissed. Posting is exception-isolated and checks `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` and app enablement; channel blocks are Android's. No replay after restart (in-memory dedup of finished cards; settled turns fire once, live).
- Known limits (accepted for launch): a final send on a card older than the newest 50 sessions posts no "Sent."; a tap on a session deleted in the same instant opens an empty conversation; quick-reply drafts never expire, so their "needs you" stays until answered, dismissed or Home is opened; Home showing another conversation counts as in front.

## Routines — 22 Nov 2026 (branch `routines`)

- Scheduled requests at a time of day, adapted from OpenPhoneUse (MIT; notice in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, text in `licenses/OpenPhoneUse-MIT.txt`, headers in adapted files).
- Consent: a routine is stored only after a tap on a card showing its schedule and exact prompt (`RoutineGate`, bound to the session and a digest of every run field, one use, 5 minutes, in memory). Removing or running one also needs a tap, so outside text cannot add, remove or start routines.
- Unattended runs: a new session per occurrence whose first turn may not use `use_app`, `open_app`, `directions`, `remember` or `routine` (hidden from the model and refused if called); the owner's own follow-up or tap restores every tool. Messages it drafts wait on the normal approval card ("Needs your OK"); nothing is sent without approval. Protected-app rules are unchanged and checked live.
- Scheduling: one exact alarm (`USE_EXACT_ALARM`, suitable for sideloaded/source builds; a Play listing would need `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` and a user grant) for the soonest occurrence; an occurrence is claimed before its run, so a repeated alarm never runs it twice; more than 15 minutes late is skipped until next time; boot, app update, time and timezone changes recompute. The alarm receiver is not exported; the exported receiver accepts only protected system broadcasts. Runs hold the foreground service and a partial wake lock (max 6 minutes) until the session settles.
- Limits: Doze networking for a multi-round run is not guaranteed by the alarm alone (phone: one-round run with the screen off while charging worked; forced deep idle not tested, see SYSTEM §5); runs after reboot start only after first unlock; model cost per run is bounded by the normal turn limits, with at most 20 routines; prompts are stored in app-private preferences (plain text, like sessions' metadata).
